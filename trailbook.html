Hiking merit badge & National Outdoor Badges

Day hikes only (back to the car by evening). Overnight trips are on the Backpacking tab. Hikes sorted into a ladder, a one-year plan that finishes the Hiking merit badge and the 100 miles for the National Outdoor Badge for Hiking, and every trip in this book matched to the six National Outdoor Badge segments.

Hiking merit badge

  • Four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, each continuous miles (short rests and one meal stop are fine; no overnight).
  • Before each: a written plan with route, gear and trail lunch, approved by the counselor.
  • After each: a short report (date, route, weather, what they saw, challenges).
  • Those hikes can count for rank but not for other merit badges.

Requirements current as of October 5, 2026.

National Outdoor Badge: Hiking

  • First Class rank.
  • Hiking or Backpacking merit badge.
  • Orienteering or Geocaching merit badge.
  • 100 total miles of hiking, backpacking, snowshoeing or cross-country skiing, including the merit badge miles.
  • Gold device for each extra 50 miles; silver for each extra 200.

National Outdoor Badges: which trips help

Each badge needs First Class rank. Trips below give the miles, nights or hours, or support a required merit badge.

Backpacking

Hiking is a day trip: out and back to the car by evening. Backpacking means sleeping on the trail and carrying all your food, water and shelter. These trips run from a first overnight to the summit of Mount Whitney, with what each trail camp actually has: sites, water, toilets, fire rings, tables, permits and a base camp nearby.

Trail camp details come from the Forest Service, NPS and trip reports, and they change after fires and storms. Call the ranger district before every trip. Backpacking miles and nights count toward the National Outdoor Badges for Hiking and Camping; overnight trips don't count as Hiking merit badge hikes.

Pack outings

Trips that work for Cub Scouts and families, roughly 150 people. Campouts are listed first, then unique overnights, then day trips. For big group camps, book the moment the reservation window opens; several have a per-site cap, so you may need two or three sites side by side.

Six years, no repeats

One outing a month from January 2027 through December 2032, matched to each place's best season. Campouts and day adventures alternate, long drives land on holiday weekends, and each July is held for summer camp (move it to June or August to match your camp week), and the rest of summer gets the big trips. Trails closed by the Eaton Fire are held until they're scheduled to reopen. Treat it as a starting draft for the patrol leaders' council.

Weekend combos

Places that sit close together, so one drive covers several adventures. Each combo counts as one outing in the 6-year plan.
