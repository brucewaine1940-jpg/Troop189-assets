Trail hikes

Day hikes only (back to the car by evening). Overnight trips are on the Backpacking tab. Here you'll find the Hiking merit badge rules, a one-year plan that finishes the badge, and every day hike: short adventure hikes with a big payoff, then training hikes up to 20-milers.

Hiking merit badge

  • Four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, each continuous miles (short rests and one meal stop are fine; no overnight).
  • Before each: a written plan with route, gear and trail lunch, approved by the counselor.
  • After each: a short report (date, route, weather, what they saw, challenges).
  • Those hikes can count for rank but not for other merit badges.

The hike ladder

Urban hikes & bike paths

Simple Saturday or Sunday treks across LA with interesting stops along the way, most starting and ending at a Metro station so you can ride home from somewhere new. Walks are 10+ miles; bike rides stay on dedicated bike paths. Walking mileages are approximate, so trace your route in a map app before you go.

Good to know

  • Metro allows bikes on trains; ride at off-peak times with a big group and split across cars.
  • A continuous 10-mile urban walk can count as a Hiking merit badge 10-miler; get the plan approved by the counselor first.
  • Bike miles count toward the National Outdoor Badge for Riding (Cycling merit badge plus 100 cycling miles).
  • Big group events to join: the Big Parade (each May, about 35 miles over two days, 80+ public stairways) and the Great LA Walk (pre-Thanksgiving weekend, about 18 miles from downtown to the ocean).

Further reading: LA guidebooks

Good places to dig for more ideas. We can't reproduce their lists, but they're worth a library hold.

  • The Best Things to Do in Los Angeles: 1001 Ideas (Joy Yoon): huge themed idea list; skim for kid-friendly and history picks.
  • 100 Things to Do in Los Angeles Before You Die (Danny Jensen & Carrie Kim): bucket-list style; good for a "patrol challenge" list.
  • An Architectural Guidebook to Los Angeles (David Gebhard & Robert Winter): building-by-building; great for planning urban walks and the Architecture merit badge.
  • Exploring Southern California: 80 Outings for the Urban Adventurer (William A. Hoffman): historic districts and lesser-known outings.
  • The Rough Guide to Los Angeles & Southern California: broad regional guide, useful for weekend combos farther out.
  • 111 Shops in Los Angeles That You Must Not Miss (Desa Philadelphia): mostly shopping; least useful for Scout outings.

Rolling windows

  • Recreation.gov wilderness and backcountry permits (Cottonwood Lakes, Boy Scout Trail): open 6 months before your date.
  • San Gorgonio (SGWA) permits: up to 90 days ahead.
  • Philmont crew treks and Cavalcade: reservations open October 1, two years before your trek.
  • National bases (Summit, Northern Tier, Sea Base): registration opens mid-January about 18 months ahead (January 2027 opens summer 2028) and fills fast.

Backpacking

Hiking is a day trip: out and back to the car by evening. Backpacking means sleeping on the trail and carrying all your food, water and shelter. These trips run from a first overnight to the summit of Mount Whitney, with what each trail camp actually has: sites, water, toilets, fire rings, tables, permits and a base camp nearby.

Trail camp details come from the Forest Service, NPS and trip reports, and they change after fires and storms. Call the ranger district before every trip. Backpacking miles and nights count toward the National Outdoor Badges for Hiking and Camping; overnight trips don't count as Hiking merit badge hikes.

Month by month

Every option in the Trailbook that's in season for the month you pick. It's a menu, not a schedule. Star ☆ the ones worth putting in front of the patrol leaders' council, then open the Shortlist to print or copy them. Trips you've marked Done (✓) are dimmed, so it's easy to spot what the troop hasn't tried yet.

Sample: what eight years with no repeats could look like

One way to use the menu: 96 months, one outing a month from January 2027 through December 2034, with no repeats, matched to each place's best season. Campouts and day adventures alternate, long drives land on holiday weekends, and each July is held for summer camp (move it to June or August to match your camp week), and the rest of summer gets the big trips. Trails closed by the Eaton Fire are held until they're scheduled to reopen. Treat it as a starting draft for the patrol leaders' council.

How devices work

  • Each segment has a gold device for every extra block (for example, every extra 50 hiking miles or 25 camping nights).
  • A silver device replaces a bigger block (for example, every extra 200 hiking miles).
  • The unit leader submits the application to the council service center.

Drive times are rough estimates from Van Nuys without traffic. Hours, prices, permits and closures change often, especially after fires, so call or check the linked source before booking.
